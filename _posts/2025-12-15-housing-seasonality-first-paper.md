This one is special to me. It started as my **STAT0035 third-year project at UCL Data Science**. The question was simple: had US housing market seasonality changed since the pandemic? The textbook said summer peak, June-July, every year. The FHFA data after 2020 looked different to me, and I wanted to know whether the eye was right.

It was. X-13-ARIMA on 33 years of FHFA HPI + Census transaction data: peak moved from May/June to March/April after 2020, amplitude grew, pattern consistent across most US regions.

What I'm proud of is that I didn't stop when the course did. After graduating I kept working on it. Yifei Huang (Northwestern) joined for the journal version. Later Cemil Selcuk at Cardiff joined to push the empirical finding into a proper structural model — that became [the companion SSRN paper]({{ '/publications/#hu2026summer' | relative_url }}).

It is rare for an undergrad project to make it through to a published journal article, let alone to grow a second paper afterwards. I'm grateful to the people who made it possible.
